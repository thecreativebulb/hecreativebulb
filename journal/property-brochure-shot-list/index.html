Three visual elements every property brochure should consider

A useful checklist for marketing teams preparing a property launch or sales brochure.

A strong property brochure needs to help a buyer understand more than the building's appearance. It should establish the overall project, show how spaces feel, and make the most relevant details easy to understand.

Plan the shoot around the brief

Start with a clear hero view that communicates the building or development in context. Add interior and amenity frames that help a prospective buyer imagine daily use. Then include details that explain quality and design intent: materials, finishes, circulation, landscape, views or shared spaces. The right mix depends on the project and audience, so agree priorities before the shoot.

Make the images work harder

A planned shot list helps avoid gaps and makes one production more useful across brochures, websites, presentations and social edits. The Creative Bulb can scope photography, film and aerial deliverables together for a consistent visual library.
